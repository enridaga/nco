1Introduction

The News Classification Ontology (NCO) v2.2. It defines the concepts needed to discuss news coverage. These concepts include Entities, Events, Situations, Categorical Topics, Claims and Viewpoints. This ontology extends nco v1.4.3 by adding characterizations of agents (persons and organizations) and further developing the modelling of claims and utterances

Preferred prefix is nco: <https://data.open.ac.uk/ontology/newsclassification/v2#>.

We import SKOS, TimeOntology

Each term below is described by its OWL 2 Manchester syntax frame, generated from the ontology source with the OWL API. Names in a frame link to their definitions; terms from other vocabularies link to their IRIs.

CategoricalTopic

http://data.open.ac.uk/ontology/newsclassification/v2#CategoricalTopic

A Categorical Topic is a broad topic for discussion and journalistic analysis that tends to be generally relevant and newsworthy. Such topics include social, economic and political issues, such as crime, poverty, taxation, finances, economic, foreign and defence policies, immigration, party politics, and many others. Broad taxonomies for journalism, such as the IPTC Media Topic NewsCodes, tend to focus on categorical topics

Class: nco:CategoricalTopic

Event

http://data.open.ac.uk/ontology/newsclassification/v2#Event

The notion of Event in NCO is based on the one used by the News Angle Ontology (Motta et al., 2020), which is in turn based on the Simple Event Model (van Hage et al., 2011). An event in this model is characterised in terms of time, location and the actors involved in the event. The relation between events and actors is mediated by actor components, which specify the role played by the actor in question in the event.

Class: nco:Event
SubClassOf:
    nco:EventOrSituation,
    nco:hasAgentComponent only nco:AgentComponent,
    nco:hasLocation only nco:Location

Omission event

http://data.open.ac.uk/ontology/newsclassification/v2#OmissionEvent

An OmissionEvent is an event capturing an expression of agency realised by not carrying out an event that was expected of the agent in question – see Payton, J.D. (2018). How to identify negative actions with positive events. Australasian Journal of Philosophy, 96(1), pp. 87-101.

Following Payton, we impose the constraint that the time of the omitted event is the same as the one associated with the omission event. That is,
OmissionEvent(?ev1) ^ :hasOmittedEvent(?ev1, ?ev2) ^ :hasTime(?ev1, ?t1) -> :hasTime(?ev2, ?t1)
This is expressed by means of a property chain specialising property :hasTime

Class: nco:OmissionEvent
SubClassOf:
    nco:NegativeEvent
     and (nco:hasOmittedEvent only nco:Event)
     and (nco:hasOmittedEvent exactly 1 nco:Event)

Used in: has omitted event

Predicted event

http://data.open.ac.uk/ontology/newsclassification/v2#PredictionEvent

We impose the constraint that the time of the predicted event has to be after that associated with the prediction event. That is:

PredictionEvent(?ev1) & hasPrediction (?ev1 ?ev_or_sit) & :hasTime (?ev1 ?t1) & :hasTime (?ev_or_sit ?t2) --> time:intervalBefore (?t1 ?t2)

This constraint is expressed through a property chain specialising time:intervalBefore

Class: nco:PredictionEvent
SubClassOf:
    nco:Event,
    nco:hasPrediction only nco:EventOrSituation,
    nco:hasPrediction exactly 1 nco:EventOrSituation

Used in: hasPrediction

Viewpoint

http://data.open.ac.uk/ontology/newsclassification/v2#Viewpoint

As pointed out in (Baden and Springer, 2017), when analysing the news discourse, we are interested in identifying viewpoints that “open up different perspectives” and “construct different meaning”. Hence, here we define a Viewpoint as a collection of claims that subscribe to the same perspective

Class: nco:Viewpoint
SubClassOf:
    nco:Collection,
    (nco:hasElement only nco:Claim)
     and (nco:hasElement min 1 nco:Claim)

Used in: Collection, hasViewpoint, includesClaim, viewpointInNewsItem

topicRole

http://data.open.ac.uk/ontology/newsclassification/v2#topicRole

It is essential to distinguish an entity, e.g., the individual J.F. Kennedy, from the associated topic, e.g., T_J.F. Kennedy. These are two different ontological objects with very different properties. For instance, while the time span of J.F. Kennedy the person was 1917-1963, the time span of the associated topic continued after his death. More in general, this relation is needed every time an individual in our knowledge base, which is not ontologically a topic (e.g., a person, an event, a statement, etc.) plays the role of a topic in the model.
